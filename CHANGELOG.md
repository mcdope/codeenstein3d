# Changelog

## Unreleased

- New: **a boss key.** `F9` drops a plain code editor over the whole page — showing the actual source file the level you are playing was generated from, syntax-highlighted — and takes everything else with it: the game pauses, fullscreen is dropped, the music stops, and the browser tab's title and icon become the file's. Press `F9` again and it all comes back, revealing the game **still paused**, so you are not dropped straight back into a fight; your next click resumes as usual. `Escape` is ignored while it is up, so a reflex press can't undo it. Two honest limits: a web page cannot minimise its own window, which is why the disguise happens inside the page; and in co-op it can only hide you, not pause the session — the same as alt-tabbing out of co-op already does


## beta-7

- New: **a guided tour the first time you open the game.** Seven popouts over the launch screen point at the controls that actually start something — the launch tabs, Demos as the zero-setup path, co-op, difficulty and gore, the file tree, the highscore board and the console. Skippable, Escape closes it, arrows move between steps, and it never appears again once dismissed. There was a static intro screen and a link to the written guide before, and neither pointed at anything you could click
- New: **locked doors are colour-coded, and so is everything that points at them.** The door in the world, its dot on the minimap and automap, the key sprite, the "You need the blue key!" message and a row of key pips on the HUD all share one colour per room — so you can see from across a room which door your key fits, and a door you cannot open yet becomes a landmark telling you where to come back to. Deliberately no yellow: amber already means a switchboard door you can just push
- New: **you can name yourself.** A "Player name" setting, remembered between sessions, that labels your runs on the leaderboard — which now has a Player column — and, in multiplayer, floats above your character in the 3D view in your own marker colour (the same colour your dot already has on the automap and minimap). It also replaces "Host"/"Guest-1" on the end-of-run comparison table. Leave it blank and everything reads as it did before
- Changed: **an Elite is a fight you can lose, not one you cannot win.** A very complex function used to become one enormous enemy — up to 50,400 health from real code — which in practice meant a wall you walked past rather than a boss you fought: across seven repositories of automated playtesting, 1,332 Elites spawned and 2 died, none of them on Hard, and every single cleared run left the Elite alive. The room now keeps its doubled health budget but splits it across a pack led by a gold-tinted Elite, so nothing in it outlasts what you can actually chew through and the incoming damage drops as you cut the pack down. The largest enemy the generator can produce fell from 50,400 to 525. Levels that had never been beaten now are: one wolf3d level went from 0 of 78 attempts to 40 of 44
- Changed: **the flamethrower thins out with distance instead of stopping dead.** Friday Hotfix had a hard 3.5-tile reach — full damage at 3.4 tiles and literally nothing at 3.6. It now does full damage to 2.5 tiles and fades to nothing by 6.5, which is a redistribution rather than a buff: the plateau ends *before* the old cutoff, paying for the extra reach. It was locked to the wrong band entirely — ordinary enemies are fought at a median 4.6 tiles, outside its old range, while only the 10-15 health nuisances sit inside it
- Changed: **a key opens its room, not one doorway.** A locked room with six ways in used to cost six keys, and coming back through a door you had already opened charged you again. Now every locked room has one coloured key that opens all of its doors, and keeps working — the genre's own idiom, and the thing this game was already dressed as. Measured on the bundled campaign: `stage06` went from five keys to two for the same two rooms
- Changed: the three runs on the shipped leaderboard now say which bot set them — **Casual**, **Gamer** and **Pro**, the playtest bot's own skill profiles. They were three anonymous rows before, with nothing to say why the scores differ
- Changed: a `TODO` comment's encounter — a trap, a mine, or a "Bug" enemy — now follows the wording of that comment, so the same TODO always produces the same thing instead of being re-rolled whenever something unrelated shifts elsewhere in the level
- Fixed: **"Watch Replay" on the shipped highscore board now plays back the run it recorded.** Every one of the three bundled replays looked wrong — the view swung to places the run never faced, the player shot at nothing and died within half a minute. The recorded runs were fine; the playback was not being told how fast the recording had been turning, so every turn came out two to five times too small and the run drifted off course in seconds. The board itself needed no re-recording, and the replays now match their own scores frame for frame
- Fixed: the shipped leaderboard was ranked backwards. It was shown in bot-profile order rather than by score, so a first-time visitor saw the *lowest* score at #1. Only ever affected that fallback board — your own runs have always been sorted
- Fixed: **a run you have cheated in says so while you are playing it.** Any cheat permanently disqualifies that run from the leaderboard — score and replay both — and the only sign of it used to be a console line printed once the run was already over. A small warning now sits on the HUD for the rest of the campaign, from the moment the cheat fires
- Fixed: **a half-loaded GitHub repo now says so.** GitHub truncates the file listing for very large repositories, and the only sign of it was a line in the browser's developer tools — so the campaign quietly played a fraction of the repo with nothing on screen to suggest anything was missing. A marker now sits above the file tree for as long as that repo is loaded, and the in-game console says the same thing, both naming the fix: clone it and use the **Local** tab
- Fixed: **running into GitHub's rate limit reads as a rate limit.** Requests are unauthenticated, so a lot of browsing eventually runs the public budget down — and every load after that failed with a bare "403 Forbidden", which reads like the repo is private. It now says you've hit the limit, how many minutes until it resets, and that a local folder works in the meantime. The same goes for a file that fails *mid-campaign*: source files are fetched one level at a time, so a repo that loaded fine can start failing partway through
- Fixed: a replay recorded on Easy or Hard was refused outright with "recorded under different game balance", even when nothing about the balance had changed. Only Normal ones could ever be watched
- Fixed: the playtest bot could not aim while dodging. One duration governed both the turn key and the movement keys, so the sidestep that fires when a fight stalls also held the turn key for a whole decision — a small aim correction executed as a rotation up to 22x larger than intended. Turn and movement now dispatch as separate phases: the same measurement went from 4,327 implausible-rotation warnings to zero, with 21% more shots fired and no extra damage taken
- Fixed: **a repository now opens on a gentle level instead of its worst one.** With no recognisable entry-point filename to go on, the game picked the *most complex* file to be level 1 — and complexity is exactly what becomes enemies and health, so it reliably opened a codebase on its most brutal map. On id-Software/wolf3d that meant starting on a 446-enemy, 13,686-tile level. It opens on the least complex file now. The bundled demo campaign never showed this, because it contains a literal `main.c` and the name match wins before any of this runs
- Fixed: **levels built almost entirely out of locked doors.** Any private method could lock a room, so a large source file turned into a key hunt: one ripgrep level carried 64 doors and 38 keys, another reached 2,653 doors and 771 keys. A level now has at most six locked doorways, picked by which rooms are worth locking — large, loot-rich, and with few ways in — and one always sits on the route to the exit, so finding the key still means something
- Fixed: **teleporters that moved you a few tiles across the room you were already in.** A `goto` used for ordinary error handling put both pads of a pair in the same room. At most one such pair per level survives now, and the cross-room pairs that genuinely shortcut a level are untouched. The bundled campaign hid how common this was — it has two pairs and both are in-room, while across real repositories 68-100% of every pair generated was, and one vim level produced 39 of them
- Fixed: **C# projects generated levels that could not be finished.** Methods on an interface were treated as private — correct inside a class, wrong inside an interface — and one Serilog file turned into 640 doors and 321 keys, a level with no solvable route through it

- Dev: a raw **event log** — one line per shot, hit, kill, drop, pickup and level boundary — behind the `?testHooks=1` gate telemetry already uses, reported by `npm run balancing:events`. Nothing was added to the render or simulation loop. It settled several questions the pre-summed counters structurally could not, including that one weapon was dead content and that "accuracy" had been conflating trigger-pulls with pellets
- Dev: `npm run balancing:budget` computes a level's enemy budget against the ammo obtainable on it without playing the level, and `npm run balancing:corpus` fetches eight real repositories pinned to tags to point it at. Between them they showed Elite health is unbounded and that fighting Edge Cases prints ammo at ~10x the damage spent
- Dev: loot rolls are reproducible via `?seed=`, so an A/B can tell a changed drop constant apart from a different roll of the same dice
- Dev: engine scalars moved into a dependency-free `combatConstants.ts`, after a hand-mirrored copy had already drifted — the playtest bot modelled its own rocket at the enemy bolt's speed, 5 tiles/sec against the real 18
- Dev: harness hardening — a wall-clock budget on `verify:multiplayer-transition`, a capture orchestrator that no longer pins the tail of a cell to a single lane, and an optional WAD download that can no longer make the project unbuildable

## beta-6

- Changed: **levels are laid out completely differently.** Rooms are placed next to each other instead of scattered and joined up in source order, so a level is a connected complex rather than rooms strung along enormous hallways. The walk from one room to the next fell from 59 tiles on average (191 at worst) to 22 (48 at worst), and a level fills over a third of its own footprint instead of a fifth
- Changed: corridors don't all look the same any more. A corridor now has a width — most a single-file squeeze, some two- or three-tile halls — long ones can step diagonally, and the shapes that interrupt them went from two to six, never the same twice in a row: 84 across the campaign instead of 277 all-but-identical ones
- Changed: levels have loops. Rooms that land near each other get an extra connecting corridor, so there's usually more than one way round instead of a single branching dead-end tree. A shortcut never opens a new way into a locked room, so it can't cost you an extra key hunt
- New: levels no longer all look alike. Each picks one of five visual stylesets — warm stone brick, rusted metal, grey tech panelling, pale marble blockwork, or the cool teal bonus levels already had — and keeps it for the whole level. Which one it gets comes from the source file, so a given file always looks the same; hazards keep their colours everywhere, and teal stays reserved for `.h` bonus levels
- Changed: a loaded WAD texture pack now skins all five stylesets separately, so real Doom textures give you the same level-to-level variety the built-in look does. A sparse pack borrows between stylesets before falling back to the built-in art, and the status line reports what each got
- New: a `switch` (or `match`) statement builds a **Switchboard** — that function's room becomes a junction with a short dead-end spur per `case`, each behind an amber push-open door and holding something small: a weak enemy, a trap, or ammo. The `default` branch gets no spur, because it's the way onward
- New: a `try`/`catch`/`finally` becomes a three-part detour — a narrow acid corridor with spike traps and a mine, an alcove of guaranteed health *and* armor at the far end, then a safe room with guaranteed loot. The acid corrodes away a couple of seconds after you step in it, so the walk back out of the dead end is free, and no dependency key ever spawns past a gauntlet — the toll stays a bet you choose to take
- New: the imports (or `#include`s, or `require`s) at the top of a file become small **Vendor Depot** alcoves in the wall of the room you start in — roughly one per four imports, stocked with ammo for the weapons you're actually holding, including the ones levels 4, 8 and 12 grant you. SMG and gas ammo can turn up there too, not only as enemy drops
- New: a function that allocates memory heavily gets a room that **leaks**. Walk in and the floor fills with acid tile by tile until you kill the enemy that function spawned, then it stops right where it is. A low warning tone and a "Memory leak — acid rising!" banner fire the moment it starts
- New: there are now two kinds of door. The blue one still needs a dependency key; the new amber one is a Switchboard's branch door and just needs a push. Both have their own colour on the minimap and automap
- Changed: a locked door tells you why it didn't open. It thumps, a "You need a key!" banner appears, and the nearest key you can actually reach pings on the minimap for a few seconds — picked by walking distance, so it never points you at one sitting behind another locked door. If every remaining key is walled off, you get the thump and the banner with nothing to follow
- Changed: every gun has its own cadence now, and none of them can be out-clicked. The Regex Shotgun is pump-action again — 0.85s between blasts, with an audible rack — and its pellets hit harder to match (18 → 25 damage each); the echo pistol caps at ~6.6 shots/sec. The cap is per player, so switching guns mid-cycle doesn't help; quick-melee on `Space` is always available
- Changed: Elite enemies are half as tough. Crossing the complexity threshold used to quadruple a room's health and put it all in one body that also hit twice as hard — one extra point took a room from four enemies with 976 health between them to a single enemy with 4000. An Elite is still clearly the harder fight, just not an unkillable wall
- Changed: monsters no longer spawn right on top of the exit marker. Enemies aren't solid, so this is about how the way out reads, not whether you can use it — what holds the exit shut is unchanged: every enemy belonging to its room has to be dead
- Fixed: the game now holds a steady 60fps where it used to sit around 44-51 and judder. Roughly one frame in three arrived late, because of *how* a handful of outlined and curved shapes were drawn — the weapon silhouette, the minimap arrow, tracers, blasts, flame jets — which are now stamped from pre-rendered images or built out of rectangles. Standing still 51.3 → 58.6fps, fighting 48.8 → **59.1**, automap open 44.3 → **59.0**, late frames down from 17-36% to under 2%
- Fixed: replays of long runs are no longer cut short. A level running past the recording limit stopped being recorded and the replay closed the gap as if it had never happened, so a full seventeen-level clear shipped as a two-level replay. The limit is now twelve minutes, up from six, and a replay that does hit it stops there instead of pretending to be complete
- Fixed: a saved replay is refused if the game's balance changed since it was recorded, instead of quietly playing a run that no longer matches its own score. The old check only covered the level's source file, but enemy health, the weapon table and constants like acid decay are generated from it rather than stored. **This invalidates every replay recorded before it**; the bundled highscore board was regenerated
- Changed: saved replays take about a third of the storage they used to, so your leaderboard is far less likely to hit the browser's storage limit and start dropping older runs. Same runs, same scores, just written more compactly — the bundled example board went from 424KB to 179KB over the wire
- Dev: the multiplayer level-transition check gates runs again instead of failing every one. It had been made advisory on the theory that the bot was unlucky, which was wrong — the failure repeated at identical coordinates across gameplay seeds. Six separate bugs, from a stuck route reporting no reason to a *completed* transition being read as a teleporter failure. The bot now fights its way to the exit when the level requires it, with combat enabled only for that fight
- Dev: bot failures are self-describing now — an anomaly names the errand it happened on, and a failure dumps the exit gate (where the bot was, which enemies belong to the exit's room, whether they're alive). Three of the six bugs above were invisible to the local harness built to reproduce them — it runs on a virtual clock, so it sees neither network delay nor the transition — and the dump found two in a single run after blind attempts had found none
- Dev: multiplayer telemetry records the bot configuration it ran under. `meta.flags` never existed in the multiplayer report, so the guard that refuses to compare runs from different bot behaviour has silently reported "not comparable" for every multiplayer A/B ever run. **Stored multiplayer runs from before this change should not be diffed against runs after it**
- Dev: the playtest bot navigates properly. It sprints down straight stretches, keeps moving while it turns instead of stopping dead at every corner, backs away from mines instead of turning into them first, re-plans mid-detour and after a fight shoves it round a corner, stops treating a door it opened as a wall, and takes keys in the order they stand in front of it. Levels finish 8-22% faster from the turning change and 27-41% faster from sprinting; planned routes are 25% shorter campaign-wide, 24-37% on the key-heavy levels. **Every pacing number this bot reported before is inflated by the standing around**
- Dev: the playtest bot fights better. It dodges while still lining up its own shot rather than only once aimed (hit 12% less, fights 12% faster, 28% more health left), steps off a bolt's flight line and sprints while doing it (ranged damage per second of exposure -25.4/-18.5/-7.9% across the profiles), picks weapons on reach and ammo economy instead of a fixed list (time-to-kill down 34-42%), and clears the enemies that keep an exit inert
- Dev: the bot's decision-making lives in `scripts/lib/combatPolicy.mjs` with unit tests — the first automated coverage that code has had — and `scripts/report-balancing-ab.mjs` fails on a survival regression between two telemetry runs. `routeEfficiencyScore` was misread as "the bot walks 3x further than it needs to" — decomposed, most of it is key fetching and deliberate loot detours — so the new `routeFollowingOverhead` measures only what navigation changes should be judged on
- Dev: the frame-time root cause was narrower than "outlines are slow": the expensive class on a GPU-accelerated canvas is geometry the rasteriser can't emit as axis-aligned quads. A filled path costs ~10ms whether you draw one or sixty-four; a rectangle outline is free unless `lineJoin` is `"round"`, where one call costs 48fps against 59 — which the weapon viewmodel set. Two earlier wrong mechanisms are retracted in `doc/dev/perf-review-2026-08-02.md`
- Dev: `perf:bench` leads with **presented frames** (dropped count and percentage) alongside busy time, which only measures the time to *record* draw calls — rasterising happens later in the GPU process. Headless rasterises in software and reads a flat 60fps with zero drops whatever the renderer does (same build: headed 59.4fps, 7/713 dropped; headless 60.0fps, 0/600), so it now warns and tags the number. `doc/dev/performance.md`'s "prefer headless" advice is rewritten and audit finding T241 should be reopened
- Dev: a CI guard (`src/engine/renderCost.test.ts`) fails the build if a per-frame renderer issues `fill()`, `stroke()` or `strokeRect()` on the scene canvas. This regression class is invisible both in review and to the headless harness, so the guard forces the pre-rendered fast path first and was verified red against a reintroduced regression
- Dev: `npm run report:level-maps` renders every campaign level's floor plan to a PNG and prints layout metrics (corridor length, longest straight run, feature spread, floor density, loop count, rooms-vs-entities). Layout quality is a visual property no assertion holds, so look at the images, not only the table. The replay recorder also reports how far past the frame cap a level ran, and `generate:default-highscore` timestamps its progress lines
- Dev: an anomaly scan against both sides of the layout change found bot qualification unchanged (3/3 on every profile) and navigation anomalies down 22%. One level got worse and is written up rather than fixed: `stage06_pipeline.py`'s acid gauntlet moved from a remote spur onto the main corridor, so the bot walks into it far more often
- Dev: the wall-texture verifier no longer asserts palette stability from sampled pixels — the frame is grabbed with the world running, so a roaming enemy in shot moved the mean between two launches of a byte-identical level, scaling with engine speed rather than with anything about the palette. Stability is now asserted on the styleset id and a grid fingerprint, both exact
- Dev: the bundled highscore board was regenerated on the newly rate-capped bot; every layout moved besides. Gamer's entry is the first full seventeen-level clear the notes record
- Dev: campaign runs are cost-bounded per combo. The orchestrator's retry loop had no invocation limit, so a combo the bot simply cannot clear would respawn work for it forever — which already happened once on the multiplayer Hard cells and had to be rescued by hand mid-run
- Dev: building the project now needs Node 22.22.2+, 24.15+ or 26+, up from 20.19+/22.12+ — jsdom 30 raised its own floor and Node 20 is end-of-life. Note the gaps: Node 23 and 25 are excluded, both odd-numbered releases that never became long-term-support versions. Nothing about the game changed
- Fixed: the startpage and every doc had the core mapping backwards. A **file** is a level and each function in it is a room — not "folder → level, file → room", which is what the intro screen, the README's headline table and the player guide all said. The game never worked the way they described
- New: a [Troubleshooting](doc/user/troubleshooting.md) page in the player guide, for everything the loader does silently — which extensions actually become levels, why `test/`, `vendor/` and `node_modules/` are skipped, the 4 MiB and binary-content limits, what happens when GitHub truncates a huge repo, and a multiplayer section covering every status message the game can show you — an expired join code, "someone else already joined", the rate limiter, a greyed-out Host tab, and which session endings are faults rather than the session simply finishing

## beta-5

- New: Multiplayer — host or join a real-time coop session (2-4 players) with a friend, no account or setup needed. Pick a campaign/workspace as usual, choose how many players you want, then share the short code your browser gives you; anyone who joins with that code connects directly to you (peer-to-peer), and more players can join the same code one at a time with nothing extra to share. A public lobby list is also available if you'd rather browse for an open session than trade a code
- Everyone in a session stays in perfect lockstep — the exact same simulation, ticking in sync — even across different browsers or devices, with automatic correction if anything ever drifts
- If a player's connection drops, the rest of the session keeps going without them after a short grace period; when the group reaches a level's exit together, a countdown gives everyone a moment to catch up before advancing
- A shared end-of-run scoreboard shows everyone's score and kills side by side. Elite enemies get tougher (more HP and damage) the more players join, and any loot dropped is visible to the whole team on the minimap/automap. Multiplayer runs don't use cheat codes, highscores, or replays — those stay single-player features
- If a strict NAT, corporate firewall, or mobile network blocks a direct connection between players, the game now falls back to a relay automatically whenever whoever's hosting the signaling server has set one up — nothing to configure on your side, and a join that used to hang on "Establishing connection…" now has a real shot at getting through
- Dev: a Docker Compose stack (`docker/`) for self-hosting the multiplayer signaling server and optional TURN relay, plus `docker/update.sh` to script routine updates — see `doc/dev/multiplayer-deployment.md`
- New: a fading "Out of ammo!" toast appears when you pull the trigger on an empty weapon, instead of nothing happening with no feedback
- Fix: the level exit didn't check whether any enemies were still alive nearby — you could walk onto it and win (or, in multiplayer, start the level-transition countdown) with a room full of live enemies behind you. The exit now stays inert until every enemy in its own room is dead, in both single-player and multiplayer
- Fix: multiplayer's FPS overlay (Right-Ctrl) always showed 0 — it now reports real numbers
- Fix: a dead multiplayer player's "YOU DIED — spectating" banner overlapped the level-transition countdown, both drawn in the same spot — the countdown now takes over cleanly
- Fix: the game canvas lost keyboard focus after every multiplayer level transition, needing a click back into the game to keep playing — the first level of a session already worked fine, only later transitions were affected
- Fix: the multiplayer end-of-run comparison table's kills and score silently reset at every level transition instead of tracking the whole run — a long, successful session could end showing 0 kills and a tiny score despite the live scoreboard climbing normally the whole time. Both now correctly reflect the full run
- Fix: disabled buttons in the multiplayer sidebar (Create/Join/Start Session) still looked fully clickable — they're now visibly greyed out with a "not allowed" cursor
- Fix: the shotgun's pellets could each individually re-trigger the kill logic for an enemy an earlier pellet in the same blast had already killed, inflating kill/score counts and occasionally triggering a false Multi Kill or Ultra Kill from a single shot

## beta-4

- Dev: a repeatable frame-time benchmark harness (`npm run perf:bench`) and chart report (`npm run perf:report`) — real-clock Playwright runs over deterministic scenarios (idle, replay-combat, particle stress, a huge GitHub-repo map, bot play) with per-frame phase timing via `?perfDebug=1` and interleaved A/B for the build flags. First full audit's verdicts: distance fog and wall-edge antialiasing are essentially free on typical maps (antialiasing costs ~0.4ms/frame on huge ones), and no engine-side hotspot reproduces the reported huge-repo framedrops — details in `perf-findings.json`
- Fix: the FPS overlay (Right-Ctrl) and the IDDQD/IDCLIP cheat toggles all silently reset every time you advanced to the next level, needing to be re-activated each time — they now carry over for the rest of the run, same as your health/ammo/weapons already did
- Wall-edge antialiasing and windowed-mode canvas resizing are both on by default now: the perf audit measured antialiasing as undetectable on normal maps (~0.4ms/frame on huge ones) and the canvas resizing as completely free on both the engine and compositing side — so you get smoother wall silhouettes, and the game window finally grows past 640×400 to fill the space your browser window actually has
- New: rebranded with a custom "CODE" wordmark logo — replaces the gun-emoji sidebar header text and the full favicon/icon set (browser tab, bookmarks, Android/iOS home screen, Windows tiles)
- New: a curated "Or pick an online texture pack" list in the sidebar, next to the existing Load WAD Texture Pack button — Freedoom (Phase 1, Phase 2, FreeDM), DOOM (Shareware), and HACX 1.2, each showing its license, credits, and a link to the source project. No download or file picker needed; fetched at build time, not committed to the repo (see the README's Credits section for full attribution)
- The 3 example runs shown in the Highscores dialog before you have any of your own are now played by the same Casual/Gamer/Pro skill levels used elsewhere (previously 3 generic, skill-blind attempts) and load much faster the first time you open that dialog — the bundled data shrank from tens of megabytes to under a megabyte
- Workspace/GitHub loads now skip `test`, `tests`, and `__tests__` directories (matched case-insensitively) alongside the existing `node_modules`/`.git`/etc. noise list, and also skip colocated test files (`foo.test.ts`, `foo_test.go`, `FooTest.java`, and similar conventions) even when they sit right next to real source rather than in a dedicated test folder — so test suites no longer generate levels
- Fix: watching a replay recorded from a GitHub repo re-fetched the whole repo with no loading indicator at all, unlike every other workspace load — it now shows the same loading screen, byte-received readout, and file-tree-scan progress the "Load GitHub repo" button already had; a failed re-fetch also now resets the UI instead of leaving the screen stuck on "Fetching…" forever
- Fix: a lore terminal kept pulsing/glowing forever, even after you'd already read it — both the in-world wall tint and the minimap marker now stop pulsing once a terminal's been interacted with at least once (it keeps its distinct wall texture in the 3D view, so it's still findable, just no longer animated)
- Fix: a corridor-breakup room's internal wall could occasionally seal off part of a level, permanently disconnecting some rooms from the exit
- Fix: moving diagonally (e.g. forward + strafe together) covered about 41% more ground per step than moving in a straight line
- Loot rebalanced: regular kills now sometimes drop nothing instead of always dropping something, and drop amounts are lower overall — ammo/health/armor felt too plentiful; a "miss" also now carries a small chance to grant the Toolchain chainsaw, giving it a real, repeatable way to unlock beyond secret rooms or a lucky Elite kill
- Easy difficulty deals more damage than before — the old curve combined with cautious play could make a run nearly unkillable
- Enemy aim now gets noticeably worse on Easy and Normal difficulty, not just their HP and damage — difficulty finally affects how "smart" enemies feel, not only how tough they are; Hard is unchanged
- Fix: a level's keys often spawned clustered near each other, all inside the biggest room reachable before opening any door — later keys are now confined to the tiles newly reached since the previous door, so they're spread across the level instead of piling up in the same starting area
- Fix: the 3 bundled example replays a first-time player sees in the Highscores dialog played back at roughly 3x real speed instead of matching the pace of the original run
- Watching a replay now returns you to the Highscores dialog once it ends (a natural win/death, stopping early, or an error), instead of dropping you back at the plain file-tree screen
- New: export a "Watch Replay" viewing as a downloadable webm video — a Record button on the replay's own transport bar, or a one-click "Export" shortcut next to each Highscores entry's "Watch" button. Recording always captures at real 1x speed and locks the transport controls for the duration
- The startpage now leads with a plain-English "New to coding?" card pointing straight at the no-setup Demos tab, before the more technical code→dungeon mapping
- New: an "Export Map as PNG" button appears once you clear a level — downloads a top-down image of it, textured with the actual walls/doors/floors/hazards you were seeing in-game (not a flat debug-style diagram), for sharing. Only ever available for a level you've already finished
- New: chaining 3 kills within 3 seconds now triggers a "Multi Kill" bonus (on-screen banner, stinger sound, and a flat score bonus); chaining 6 within 6 seconds triggers a bigger "Ultra Kill" instead
- Fix: Extreme gore mode's blood particles could render at nearly twice the canvas height at point-blank range — particle size and particle count are now capped across every gore tier, not just Extreme
- Dev: the browser tab title now stamps on the exact git ref of the running build (a real release tag if HEAD is tagged, otherwise a short commit hash), instead of just a build timestamp — makes a stale cached build after a deploy obvious at a glance

## beta-3

- Texture-mapped walls, doors, floors, lore terminals, hazard (acid) floors, teleporter pads, and spike traps — procedural defaults, or real textures/flats sourced from a loaded DOOM `.wad` file
- Fix: holding Toolchain's melee-fire (previously Left-Ctrl) while also moving forward could close the entire browser — `Ctrl+W` is a browser-reserved "close tab" shortcut page JavaScript cannot block.
