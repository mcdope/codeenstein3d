# Tips

[← Back to index](README.md)

- **Corners break line-of-sight.** Enemies need to actually see you to aggro from a distance, so ducking around a corner can keep a roaming enemy from ever noticing you — but landing even one shot on them aggros instantly no matter the range, so a missed opening shot has consequences.
- **The knife is a real fallback, not just a panic button.** It's infinite ammo and heals a sliver per kill — if you're dry on bullets, don't be afraid to lean on it rather than hoarding a last few rounds.
- **The Toolchain chainsaw is a strict upgrade over the knife, not a sidegrade.** Once you find it (a secret room or an Elite kill, from campaign level 4 on) it permanently replaces the knife on Space — double the damage, a bigger lifesteal heal, and it revs continuously while held instead of one swing per press. There's no forced unlock for it, though, so a loot-unlucky run can finish without ever finding one.
- **Reload between fights, not during one.** A reload only ever *moves* ammo — nothing is lost by topping up a half-empty gun — so `R` on the way to the next room is free, and dry-firing into an automatic reload mid-fight is the expensive way to find that out. The shotgun is where it bites hardest: two shells, then a reload longer than its own pump. If one does catch you at the wrong moment, switching weapons cancels it instantly, and quick-melee on `Space` never stops working.
- **An Elite's shot is the one you can dodge.** Every enemy fires its own bolt now, and the colour tells you what's coming before it lands. The slow orange shell is an Elite's, it's rare, and it hurts more than anything else in the game — about half your Stability in one hit, and on Hard two of them will kill you from full — but it travels slowly enough to step out of the way of, so treat it as a thing to react to rather than absorb. Magenta is a regular enemy's, and fast cyan is Edge Case chip damage you can mostly ignore while you deal with something bigger.
- **A spotted mine isn't automatically a dead end.** Once you've seen one, you can shoot it from outside its blast radius to clear it safely instead of trying to tiptoe around it.
- **Keys come in an order — follow the one you can reach.** A locked door won't always ping its own key, because that key is often sitting inside a room some *other* key opens. When that happens the banner names the one to fetch first and the minimap pings that instead. It's never a dead end, so don't go hunting a colour you haven't seen: take the lead you're given and the chain unwinds itself.
- **You won't always need every key on the level.** Whether the exit is behind a locked room varies wildly — measured across a corpus of real repositories, of the levels showing two or more key pips, about a quarter need *none* of them to reach the exit and about a quarter need *all* of them. The rest are somewhere between. So a level full of keys isn't necessarily a level-long key hunt; if you just want out, try the exit before clearing every locked room.
- **Fake walls aren't perfectly invisible, but the map won't help you find them.** Secret rooms sit behind walls with a very slight color tint compared to the real thing in the normal 3D view — if a wall looks *almost* right, it's worth pressing `F` on it. Neither the corner minimap nor the Tab automap gives any hint of where one is; that's deliberate.
- **Corridors are where trap variety lives.** Choke points get the spike traps and mines; open rooms generally don't — and a corridor that opens into a wider hall stops being a choke point, so the narrow stretches and the pinch in a gateway are where to expect them.
- **Acid in a `try` gauntlet burns out; acid anywhere else doesn't.** Step through an Exception Handling Zone and the tiles you crossed corrode away behind you, so the trip back with the loot is clear. A global variable's acid pool is permanent terrain and will hurt you every single time.
- **Take the other way round.** Levels usually have more than one route between rooms now. If a corridor is defended, there's often a loop that isn't.
- **Reading, exploring, and finding secrets all pay real points.** Every unique lore terminal you read adds a flat bonus, every secret room you open adds double that, and clearing 95%+ of a level's walkable area adds a large one — worth a detour if you're not in a rush.
- **Header files are restock stops.** If you land on a `.h`-derived bonus level, loot odds are noticeably better than normal — a good place to top off before pushing on.
- **Difficulty cuts both ways.** Hard doesn't just make enemies tougher — it also thins out how much ammo/health/swap you find, so ammo discipline matters more the higher you go.
- **A level that outguns you isn't the end of the run.** Difficulty comes straight from the source file, and the game already avoids starting you on the hardest map in a repository — which means some file in there *is* the hardest. On a big enough one, the ammunition lying around genuinely doesn't cover everything that spawned. Two things to know before you write it off. First, try the exit anyway: it only needs *its own room* cleared, so a fight you can't afford isn't automatically a level you can't leave. Second, if it really is beyond one player's ammunition, that's what co-op is for — every player who joins brings their own guns and their own starting ammunition, while the level's pickups stay the one pile they always were, and the ordinary roster doesn't grow to match. The team also only loses the level on a full wipe, and whoever reaches the exit takes everyone with them. See [Multiplayer § What's different](multiplayer.md#whats-different-in-multiplayer). (This is about the ammo economy, not routing — a locked door is still never a dead end, and no single enemy is ever a sponge you can't chew through.)

- **Cyan enemies aren't worth taking cover from.** Edge Cases (small, jarringly cyan, found in the widenings and alcoves dressed into a corridor, and occasionally mixed into a `switch`-heavy room's pack) hit for a fraction of a normal enemy's damage and go down in two pistol rounds — their erratic darting makes them mildly annoying to line up, but they're a nuisance to swat, not a threat to plan around.
- **Standing on the exit does nothing until its room is clear.** If you reach it and nothing happens, an enemy that spawned in that same room is still alive somewhere — there's no on-screen warning for this, so if the exit seems unresponsive, backtrack and finish the fight.
- **You can build your own level on purpose.** Since a level is generated straight from a file's actual code structure, you can write (or ask a coding agent to write) a source file specifically to get the enemies/hazards/secrets you want — see [Designing Your Own Levels](level-design.md).
